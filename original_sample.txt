Navarino battle {20/10/1827}: a battle between the Ottoman Empire and the allied forces of Britain, France, and Russia, resulting in the destruction of the Ottoman-Egyptian fleet and weakening Ottoman control over Algeria.
French invasion of Algeria {05/07/1830}: France launched a military expedition against Algeria under the pretext of a diplomatic incident involving the Dey of Algiers, marking the beginning of 132 years of French colonization.
Proclamation of November 1st {01/11/1954}: the official launch of the Algerian War of Independence, marked by coordinated FLN attacks across the country, known as Toussaint Rouge, signaling the beginning of the armed struggle for liberation.
Massacre of Setif and Guelma {08/05/1945}: French colonial forces and settler militias massacred tens of thousands of Algerian civilians following nationalist demonstrations at the end of World War II, a pivotal turning point in the independence movement.
Battle of Algiers {07/01/1957}: a major urban guerrilla campaign launched by the FLN inside Algiers, countered by brutal French military operations including widespread use of torture, becoming a defining and internationally recognized moment of the revolution.
Evian Accords {18/03/1962}: peace agreements signed between France and the GPRA in Evian-les-Bains, officially ending the Algerian War and paving the way for Algerian self-determination after years of brutal conflict.
Independence of Algeria {05/07/1962}: Algeria officially gained independence from France following the Evian Accords, ending 132 years of colonization after a war costing over one million Algerian lives and displacing millions more.
Nationalization of Oil Industry {24/02/1971}: President Boumediene announced the nationalization of Algerian oil and gas resources, asserting full sovereignty over the country's natural wealth and reshaping the national economy around hydrocarbons.
Black October Riots {05/10/1988}: widespread popular protests erupted across Algeria against the single-party government and deteriorating living conditions, leading to violent crackdowns and eventually prompting political reforms and a new constitution.
First Multiparty Elections {12/06/1990}: Algeria held its first multiparty local elections since independence, resulting in a landslide victory for the Islamic Salvation Front and marking a dramatic shift in the country's political landscape.
Cancellation of Elections {11/01/1992}: the Algerian military cancelled the second round of parliamentary elections after the FIS dominated the first round, triggering a decade-long civil conflict known as the Black Decade.
Battle of Ain Temouchent {20/08/1955}: a series of coordinated FLN attacks in northeastern Algeria organized by Zighout Youcef, marking a major escalation of the revolution and provoking severe French reprisals against the civilian population.
Fall of Constantine {13/10/1837}: French forces under General Vallee captured the city of Constantine after a second siege, consolidating French military control over eastern Algeria following the failed first siege of 1836.
Emir Abdelkader {1808-26/05/1883}= Algerian political and religious leader who led armed resistance against French colonization for over fifteen years, founder of the first Algerian state, celebrated for his chivalry and humanitarian principles even toward enemies.
Larbi Ben M'hidi {1923-04/03/1957}= Algerian revolutionary and founding member of the FLN, one of the most charismatic and principled leaders of the revolution, arrested by French paratroopers and executed under General Massu's orders in secret.
Fatma N'Soumer {1830-09/07/1863}= Algerian female resistance figure known as the Jeanne d'Arc of Algeria, led Kabyle tribes against French forces in the mountains of Kabylie and became a powerful symbol of resistance and dignity.
Mohamed Boudiaf {1919-29/06/1992}= 1808 founding member of the FLN and one of the nine historic leaders of the revolution, returned from exile to become President of Algeria in 1992 and was assassinated during a public speech in Annaba.
Didouche Mourad {1927-18/01/1955}= one of the nine historic founders of the FLN and leader of the North Constantine zone, killed in battle against French forces near Conde-Smendou only months after launching the revolution.
Mostefa Ben Boulaïd {1917-22/03/1956}= one of the nine historic founders of the FLN and leader of the Aures wilaya, a key architect of the November 1st offensive, killed by an exploding radio trap planted by French intelligence services.
Ahmed Ben Bella {1916-11/04/2012}= first President of independent Algeria and one of the nine historic founders of the FLN, imprisoned by France from 1956 to 1962 after his plane was intercepted, later overthrown in a coup by Houari Boumediene in 1965.
Houari Boumediene {1932-27/12/1978}= Algerian revolutionary and statesman who served as President from 1965 until his death, nationalized the oil industry in 1971, championed third-world causes globally and built Algeria into a major non-aligned movement leader.
Ferhat Abbas {1899-24/12/1985}= prominent Algerian nationalist politician and first President of the Provisional Government of the Algerian Republic GPRA, initially advocated for equal rights within France before joining the armed revolution after the Setif massacres.
Zighout Youcef {1921-25/09/1956}= FLN commander of the North Constantine zone who organized the massive 20 August 1955 offensive, significantly expanding the revolution's reach and popular support, killed during French military operations in the Skikda region.
Ali La Pointe {1930-08/10/1957}= FLN fighter and key operative during the Battle of Algiers under Saadi Yacef, a former street criminal turned committed revolutionary, killed when French forces detonated explosives at his hideout in the Casbah of Algiers.
Krim Belkacem {1922-18/10/1970}= one of the nine historic founders of the FLN and powerful military and political leader of the revolution, represented the GPRA at the Evian negotiations, later assassinated in Frankfurt under mysterious circumstances.
Abane Ramdane {1920-27/12/1957}= key political architect of the Algerian Revolution who organized the Soummam Congress in 1956, establishing the political and organizational framework of the FLN, secretly assassinated by fellow FLN leaders in Morocco.
Soummam Congress {20/08/1956}: a landmark internal congress of the FLN held secretly in the Soummam valley of Kabylie, establishing the organizational hierarchy of the revolution and producing a foundational political platform prioritizing the political over the military.
Morice Line Construction {1957-1958}: France constructed a heavily electrified and mined barrier stretching hundreds of kilometers along the Tunisian and Moroccan borders to cut off FLN fighters inside Algeria from external supply and reinforcement networks.
Algerian Constitution {08/09/1963}: Algeria adopted its first post-independence constitution, establishing a single-party presidential republic under the FLN, concentrating power in the presidency and laying the ideological foundation of the new Algerian state.
Chadli Bendjedid {1929-06/10/2012}= third President of Algeria who succeeded Boumediene, oversaw a period of economic liberalization and political opening in the late 1980s, resigned in January 1992 amid the electoral crisis and rising civil conflict.
Tahar Djaout {1954-02/06/1993}= celebrated Algerian writer, poet, and journalist, founder of the weekly magazine Ruptures, assassinated by Islamist extremists at the beginning of the Black Decade, becoming one of its most mourned intellectual victims.
Liamine Zeroual {1941-}: Algerian military officer and statesman who served as President from 1994 to 1999, oversaw a period of intense civil conflict during the Black Decade and initiated dialogue with armed groups before organizing presidential elections in 1995.
Abdelaziz Bouteflika {1937-17/09/2021}= longest-serving President of Algeria from 1999 to 2019, implemented the Civil Concord and National Reconciliation policies that ended the Black Decade, and resigned after mass protests of the Hirak movement demanded an end to his fifth term bid.
Hirak Movement {22/02/2019}: a massive and unprecedented popular protest movement erupted across Algeria opposing President Bouteflika's bid for a fifth term, leading to his resignation in April 2019 and demanding sweeping democratic reforms and an end to military-backed governance.
